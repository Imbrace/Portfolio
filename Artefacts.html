Project Artefacts

A selection of interactive digital artefacts developed as part of research projects, teaching, public engagement and interdisciplinary collaborations. These projects demonstrate my work across virtual reality, game-based learning, location-aware systems, machine learning and digital heritage.

Unconventional Convention

This virtual reality experience challenged participants to solve four interconnected puzzles aboard a spaceship rapidly descending into a black hole.

I served as the project lead, mentoring students I taught as they designed prototype puzzle concepts for the experience. I subsequently refined and integrated each student's work into a cohesive and fully realised VR experience.

The project was showcased at the Unconventional Convention, an event hosted by Coventry University in collaboration with Glued Marketing and Risk Evolves at the university's Delia Derbyshire building.

The experience was presented as a cooperative escape room inspired by games such as Don't Drop the Bomb. A single player in VR navigated the puzzles while other participants, outside the headset, provided guidance using a physical guidebook.

PGR VR Research Showcase

This project was created to present my PhD research at an immersive-themed research event at Coventry University.

Instead of using a traditional poster or static display, I designed and rendered a virtual reality environment that functioned as a digital exhibition space.

The VR experience presented key findings from my research and allowed me to deliver a short virtual presentation within the environment, offering attendees a more engaging and interactive way to explore the work.

TrainMate

This project investigates the integration of sensor-driven data within game-based learning systems.

As part of the work, I designed and implemented a location-aware gamified application that leverages real-world map data and real-time location tracking to monitor users' train journeys.

Players are able to collect virtual stamps at visited stations, using physical movement as a core interaction mechanic.

Unity Location-Aware 2025

EmotionBots

EmotionBots is a prototype turn-based RPG that explores facial recognition as a core gameplay mechanic.

Players control robots that draw power from their owners' emotional expressions, using these emotions to influence combat against opposing robots.

I developed a Python-based Flask server that captures the player's facial input, evaluates emotional expressions using a trained machine-learning model, and outputs confidence scores.

These values are then transferred into the game and used to determine both the elemental type of each attack and the resulting damage.

Gallery of Living History Card Game

The Gallery of Living History (GoLH) is a non-profit organisation dedicated to exploring themes of history, memory and culture through research and creative practice.

I was tasked with transforming their analogue "Top Trumps"-style card game into a digital version. The original game featured artwork of historical figures created by children from partner schools.

The resulting game was designed for distribution on both PC and mobile platforms and intended for use at public events.
